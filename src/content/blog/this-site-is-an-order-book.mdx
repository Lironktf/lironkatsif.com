Most personal sites have a contact form. Mine has a matching engine's little cousin.

The centerpiece of this site is **LIRON/USDC** — a live order book where the
asset is my time. It works like any book you'd see on an exchange, just
smaller and more honest:

- **The asks (red)** are real. Each level is a stack of 15-minute blocks I've
  decided to sell at that price — starting at fifty cents, because let's be
  honest about my market cap — straight from a JSON file I edit. Click one and pay in
  USDC — connect a wallet (Phantom, Solflare, whatever speaks Wallet
  Standard) and trade in one click, or scan a Solana Pay QR. Either way the
  site watches the chain for your transfer — when it confirms, the trade
  prints on the tape and the ask size drops.
- **The bids (green)** are indicative. Name a price below the market and the
  bid rests in the book (and lands in my inbox). I fill the ones I like.
  Sometimes the spread is where the interesting conversations happen.
- **The tape** is the receipt log. No fake volume: if the book looks quiet,
  it's because it is. There's a *demo feed* toggle that streams simulated
  activity so you can watch the book move — everything it generates is
  tagged `demo`.

## Why

Pricing your time is awkward, so most people don't do it in public. But a
posted price is information: it tells you exactly when it's worth someone's
while to talk to me, and it tells me exactly what my weekend priority rate
should be. A one-sided price felt incomplete, though. Markets have two sides.
Letting visitors bid means the price of my time is negotiated in the open,
the way it should be.

Also — and I want to be honest about the ordering of motivations here — an
order book is just a cooler hero section than a headshot.

## How

The site is [Astro](https://astro.build) with exactly one React island: the
book. Everything else is static HTML. The Solana machinery
(`@solana/web3.js`, `@solana/pay`) is lazy-imported only when you open a buy
ticket, so reading this post costs you zero web3 bytes.

Payments are direct wallet-to-wallet USDC transfers on Solana — no payment
processor, no fees, no custody. Each checkout generates a unique reference
key that gets attached to the transfer, and the client polls RPC until a
transaction carrying that reference lands. `validateTransfer` then checks
the recipient, mint and amount before the site calls it a fill.

A note on what this isn't: it's not a token, there's nothing to flip, and
buying a block entitles you to exactly one thing — fifteen minutes. The SEC
has enough on its plate.

## The look

The design brief was two words: *Bloomberg, blueprint.* Near-black with one
amber accent, Geist Mono with tabular figures for every number, hairline
borders, a faint dot grid, scanlines on the terminal panel. Green and red
appear only where they mean something — bid and ask.

If you'd like to argue about any of this, the market is right there.
Cheapest hour wins.
